When translating a PDF helps

Received a contract, a manual, a medical summary or a letter from abroad in a language you don't read? Translating the whole PDF at once, with the layout kept, is much faster than copying paragraphs into a translation site one by one. It works in both directions, so you can also send an English version of a Hebrew document.

Keep the layout, or plain text?

With Keep original layout on, translated text is placed where the original text was, so tables and headings stay in place. Translations are often longer than the source, especially from Hebrew to English, so text in small boxes may get tight. If the result looks cramped, run it again with layout off for a clean, easy-to-read document.

What to expect from machine translation

Machine translation is good for understanding a document and getting the details right in everyday texts. It can miss legal nuance, industry terms and names. For anything official, like court documents or certificates for immigration, use a certified translator and treat this version as a draft.

Scanned documents

A scanned PDF has no text to translate. Run it through OCR with the Searchable PDF output first, then translate the result.
