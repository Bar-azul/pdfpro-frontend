When you need PDF to PowerPoint

Someone sent you slides as a PDF and you need to present them, add a few slides of your own, or reorder them. Converting to PowerPoint puts each page on its own slide, so you can present in full screen, drag slides around and mix them into your own deck.

How the conversion works

Each page is placed on a slide as a high-quality image. That's why the result looks exactly like the PDF, including fonts, charts and Hebrew text, with nothing shifting. The trade-off is that the text on those slides can't be edited directly. You can still add text boxes, shapes and notes on top, or add new slides between them.

Need editable text?

If you need to change the wording, use PDF to Word to get editable text, then paste it into your slides.

Keeping the file size down

Standard quality is enough for a projector or a video call. Very high is only worth it for printing or very large screens, and makes a much bigger file.

How to present slides you received as a PDF

  1. Convert the PDF to PowerPoint here and open the file.
  2. In slideshow mode (F5 on a computer) each page fills the screen, and you move between slides with the arrow keys or a clicker.
  3. You can add speaker notes to each slide and see them in Presenter View while the audience only sees the slide.

If you only need to present without changing anything, there's also a way without converting, explained in the guide to presenting a PDF as slides.

Common problems and fixes

  • The deck is too big for email: convert again at 150, or share the file as a Google Drive or OneDrive link.
  • Black bars at the sides: the PDF isn't the same shape as the screen (an A4 page versus a widescreen display). Under Design → Slide Size you can pick the closer ratio.
  • You need to change a number or word on a slide: cover the area with a white rectangle and type a new text box on top.
  • Google Slides or Keynote: both open the PowerPoint file you get here.
