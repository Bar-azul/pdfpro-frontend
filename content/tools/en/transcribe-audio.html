When it helps

  • Long voice messages: a five-minute WhatsApp voice note becomes text you can read in ten seconds, search and copy from.
  • Calls and meetings: a call with a lawyer, an accountant or a landlord, or a recorded team meeting, becomes a written record. No need to listen again to find what was said.
  • Lectures and interviews: students, journalists and researchers get text with timestamps, so it's easy to jump back to the exact moment in the recording.
  • Video subtitles: choose SRT and upload the file to YouTube, Facebook or a video editor. Most social video is watched with the sound off.

How to get a more accurate transcript

  • Pick the spoken language. Automatic detection works, but setting the language is more accurate, especially for short recordings.
  • Record close to the speaker. A phone on the table facing the speaker beats a phone in a pocket or across the room.
  • Cut background noise. Air conditioning, music and traffic hurt recognition more than anything else.
  • Check names and numbers. First names, street names and amounts are where most mistakes happen. The timestamp next to each paragraph makes it easy to go back and listen.

Longer than 60 minutes?

Cut it into parts in any audio editor or phone app and transcribe each part. You can then combine the Word documents, or save them as PDFs and join them with Merge PDF.

After transcribing

Need the transcript in another language? Save the Word document as PDF and translate it with Translate PDF. Want to send the summary as a file that can't be edited? Word to PDF.
