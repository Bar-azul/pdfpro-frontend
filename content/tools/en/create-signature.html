What "digital signature" means here

In everyday use, a digital signature is any signature added to a document without pen and paper. This tool creates the signature itself as an image: a PNG with a transparent background, so it looks pen-signed on any background, white, colored or on a form. There is also the certified kind, a digital signature backed by an identity certificate. The differences are explained in the guide to signature types.

How to add the signature to a document

  • Word: Insert → Pictures → choose the signature file. Then click the image → Wrap Text → In Front of Text, and drag it onto the signature line.
  • Google Docs: Insert → Image → Upload from computer, then "In front of text" in the menu under the image.
  • Gmail signature: Settings → General → Signature → the image icon, and upload the file.
  • A PDF: the simplest way is Sign PDF, which adds the signature and the date to the file in one step.

Tips for a signature that looks right

  • Draw it large. A signature that uses the whole width of the box comes out smoother. The file is cropped around the signature automatically.
  • On a phone, turn it sideways. You get a bigger box and a more natural stroke.
  • Blue or black? Blue shows it's an original signature rather than a copy; black suits forms that will be printed or photographed.
  • Keep the file. You can reuse the same image whenever you need to sign.

Privacy

The signature is made entirely in your browser. It isn't sent to a server, isn't stored by us and isn't seen by anyone. Only you get the file.
