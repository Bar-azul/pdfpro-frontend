When you need PDF to JPG

Images are easier to share than PDFs in many places. Social networks, messaging apps and website builders often don't accept PDF files, and a phone shows an image instantly without opening a separate app. Converting pages to images is useful for posting a flyer or menu, sending a single page on WhatsApp, or putting a page into a presentation or document.

Choosing format and quality

  • JPG gives smaller files and suits pages with photos and colors.
  • PNG keeps text, tables and thin lines crisp, which suits documents and diagrams.
  • High quality looks sharp on any screen. Use Print quality only when you're going to print, because the files get much larger.

Long documents

Every page becomes a separate image, so a 40-page PDF gives you 40 files. If you only need a few pages, extract them first with Split PDF.

Going the other way

To turn images back into a PDF, use JPG to PDF.

Which quality to choose

  • 100 DPI: small files, enough for viewing on a phone and sending in WhatsApp.
  • 150 DPI: a good default for a computer screen, a website or slides.
  • 300 DPI: for printing, or to read small print in a scanned document. Files are about four times larger than at 150.

Common problems and fixes

  • Text looks blurry: choose PNG at 150 or 300 DPI. JPG compresses and slightly blurs small text.
  • WhatsApp shrank the image: WhatsApp compresses images sent as photos. Send it as a "document" to keep the quality.
  • You need just one page of a long file: take it out first with Split PDF, so you don't get dozens of images.
  • You want part of a page: convert the page to an image, then crop it in your phone's gallery or any image editor.

Image or PDF for sending?

For an official submission, like a form for a bank or insurer, keep the PDF: it holds all the pages together and its text stays searchable. An image suits quick sharing, social posts and inserting into a document or slides.
