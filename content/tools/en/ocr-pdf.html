What OCR does

A scan or a photo of a page is just a picture. You can read it, but a computer can't: you can't search it, copy from it, or edit it. OCR (optical character recognition) reads the letters in the image and turns them back into real text.

Hebrew OCR

Hebrew OCR is harder than English. Letters like ו, ז and ן look alike, and many documents mix Hebrew with English words and numbers. Choose Hebrew + English for anything mixed, such as invoices, forms and letters with email addresses. Choose Hebrew alone for purely Hebrew text, which slightly improves accuracy.

Which output to choose

  • Plain text to copy a paragraph or a number quickly.
  • Word document to edit the content.
  • Searchable PDF to keep the original look while making the text searchable and copyable, useful for archiving scanned documents.

Getting accurate results

  • Photograph the page flat, straight on, and in good light. Shadows and angles are the most common cause of errors.
  • Crop out the background, like the table or your fingers.
  • If a scan is sideways, fix it first with Rotate PDF.
  • Handwriting isn't recognized reliably. OCR works best with printed text.
