Need to send a signed form, a receipt or a certificate, and there's no scanner at home? Your phone is all you need. This guide explains how to photograph a document so it looks like a real scan, and how to turn the photos into a single PDF.

Why not just send the photo?

You can, and sometimes that's enough. But in most cases a PDF is better:

  • Many upload forms, from government offices, health providers, insurers and employers, only accept PDF.
  • A multi-page document stays one tidy file instead of five images arriving in random order.
  • An A4 PDF prints like a normal document, without black margins or cropping.

How to take the photo

The difference between an amateur-looking photo and a clean scan is mostly in how you shoot:

  • A dark, flat surface. A white page on a dark table gives clear edges that are easy to crop.
  • Natural light, no flash. Flash creates a bright spot in the middle of the page. Next to a window in daylight is ideal.
  • Straight from above. Hold the phone parallel to the page. Shooting at an angle turns the page into a trapezoid and blurs the text on the far side.
  • No shadow from your hand or phone. If there is one, move so the light comes from the side.
  • The whole page in the frame. Better to leave a margin and crop later than to cut off a corner of the text.

Crop and straighten

Before converting, open each photo in your phone's gallery and crop it to the edges of the page. Both iPhone and Android have a built-in crop and straighten tool in the photo editor, which also fixes a page that came out crooked.

Using the phone's built-in scanner

Most phones can also scan straight to PDF, cropping and straightening the page for you:

  • iPhone: in the Files app, tap the ⋯ button and choose Scan Documents. Hold the phone over the page and it captures automatically. Add more pages, tap Save, and you get a PDF in Files. The Notes app has the same option under the camera or paperclip icon.
  • Android: in the Google Drive app, tap the + button and choose Scan. Each page is cropped automatically, and the result is saved to Drive as a PDF. On many Samsung and Pixel phones the camera app also offers a scan button when it sees a document.

If your phone doesn't have this, a regular photo cropped by hand, as above, works just as well.

Turn it into a PDF

  1. Open JPG to PDF, right from your phone.
  2. Select all the photos of the document.
  3. Put them in page order with Move up.
  4. Choose A4 as the page size and download.

For two-sided documents like an ID card, photograph both sides and put them in the same file, front first.

File too large?

A photo from a recent phone can weigh several megabytes, so a five-page document can exceed an upload limit. Run the PDF through Compress PDF. Medium is almost always right, and the text stays readable.

Want the text to be searchable?

A PDF made from photos is still an image: you can't copy text from it or search it. If that matters, for example for a document archive, run the file through OCR and choose the Searchable PDF output.

Sensitive documents
