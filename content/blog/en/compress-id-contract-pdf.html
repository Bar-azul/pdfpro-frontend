A bank, insurer or government site asks you to attach a copy of your ID or a signed contract, then rejects the file because it's too big. Compression fixes that, but with these documents there's a trap: compress too hard and you blur exactly what the clerk needs to see, the digits of an ID number and the lines of a signature. Here's how to shrink the file and keep everything readable.

Why the file is so big

An ID or a signed contract almost always arrives as a scan or a phone photo, which means an image. A phone camera shoots 12 megapixels or more, far more than you need to read an A4 page. So a five-page photographed contract can weigh 15–20 MB, while most sites cap uploads at 5 or 10 MB.

How to compress without losing legibility

  1. Open Compress PDF and choose the file.
  2. Choose Medium. For a document with a signature or an ID number, don't start at the maximum.
  3. Download it; the result tells you by how much the file shrank.
  4. If it's still over the limit, try High, and check again (below).

What the tool protects

  • Signatures and stamps added as transparent images aren't recompressed. Ordinary compression sometimes turns them into a black box or gives them a background. Here they're left alone.
  • Typed text isn't re-encoded. In a contract made on a computer and then signed, the clauses stay exactly as sharp.
  • Images are scaled to the size they're shown on the page, not to one size for all. A scan that fills the page keeps enough detail to read and print.
  • If compression doesn't help, you get the original. If the result isn't smaller or is missing pages, the original comes back with a note.

A one-minute check before you send

Open the compressed file and zoom into three places:

  1. The ID number and date of birth: every digit should be clear. 3 and 8, and 1 and 7, are the first to blur.
  2. The signature: the line should be continuous, without "steps" or smudges around it.
  3. The smallest text on the page, usually footnotes in a contract. If those are readable, everything else is.

If anything is blurry, go back to the original and compress at a lower level. Don't compress an already compressed file: every round adds blur.

Still too big?

  • Remove pages you don't need, such as the blank back of a card or an appendix nobody asked for, with Split PDF.
  • Send it in parts. If the form allows several attachments, split the contract in two.
  • Rescan in black and white or greyscale at 200–300 DPI. That's much smaller than a colour photo and stays sharp.
