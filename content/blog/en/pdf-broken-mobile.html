The PDF looks perfect on your computer, but whoever opens it on a phone sees boxes instead of letters, a page lying on its side, or a file that won't load at all. Almost always it's one of five known problems, and each has a one-minute fix. Here's how to recognise each and what to do.

1. Boxes or odd characters instead of text

What you see: empty boxes, question marks, or a completely different font that wrecks the layout.

Why: the font isn't embedded in the file. The computer that made the PDF has the font; the phone doesn't, and has nothing to replace it with.

The fix: recreate the PDF from the original document. In Word's save-as-PDF options, tick "embed fonts". Without Word, Word to PDF embeds the fonts in the file. If all you have is the PDF, turn it into images with PDF to JPG and back into a PDF: the text won't be searchable any more, but it will look the same on every device.

2. The file won't load or freezes

What you see: a white screen, endless loading, or a message that the file is too large to preview in WhatsApp or email.

Why: very high-resolution scans and photos. Five photographed pages can weigh 20 MB or more.

The fix: run it through Compress PDF at medium. Scans usually shrink a lot with no visible difference on a phone screen. More in PDF too large? How to shrink it.

3. A page on its side or upside down

What you see: you have to turn the phone to read, or one page is upside down in an otherwise fine document.

Why: the scanner or camera app saved the page the wrong way round. Some desktop viewers fix that on screen rather than in the file, so only other people see the problem.

The fix: Rotate PDF saves the correct orientation inside the file, so it opens right everywhere. There's a guide for this.

4. Reversed or scrambled Hebrew

What you see: words in reverse order, numbers and brackets in the wrong place, or paragraphs stuck to the left.

Why: the file was made by software that stores Hebrew incorrectly. One PDF viewer copes, another doesn't, so it only looks fine on your computer.

The fix: recreate the PDF from the source with Word or Word to PDF, not an old "PDF printer". The details are in why Hebrew gets scrambled in PDFs.

5. The file asks for a password or allows nothing

What you see: the phone asks for a password, or the file opens but can't be saved, printed or shared.

Why: the file is protected. Your computer may have the password saved in the browser; the phone doesn't.

The fix: if the recipient doesn't need protection, remove it before sending with Unlock PDF. If they do, send them the password in a separate message. More in password-protected PDFs.

The check that saves all of this

Before sending an important PDF, such as a CV, a quote or a document for an application, send it to yourself on WhatsApp and open it on your phone. Ten seconds of checking saves an awkward "I can't open your file" email.
