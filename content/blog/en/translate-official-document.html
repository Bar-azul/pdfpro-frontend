You received a document in a language you don't read well, from an employment contract at an international company to a letter from a foreign university or hospital, and you need to understand exactly what it says. Machine translation of the whole PDF gives you the full picture in a minute. But there are cases where it isn't enough, and it's worth knowing which.

When machine translation is enough

  • Understanding a document you received: what's being asked of you, the terms, the deadlines.
  • Preparing for a conversation with a lawyer, an accountant or a doctor.
  • Informal documents: product manuals, correspondence, general information.
  • A first draft that a human translator will review later.

When you need a human translator

  • Documents submitted to an authority: immigration offices, courts, foreign institutions. They usually require a certified or notarized translation, and machine translation won't be accepted.
  • Contracts you're signing: legal wording is full of nuance. One word can change an obligation.
  • Medical documents used for decisions: machine translation is fine for a general understanding, but medical decisions are made with a doctor.

Even in these cases, machine translation helps: it lets you understand roughly what's written before you go to a translator, so you can ask the right questions.

How to translate a PDF

  1. Open Translate PDF and upload the file.
  2. Choose the target language. The source language is detected automatically.
  3. Leave Keep original layout on, so tables and headings stay in place.
  4. Download the translated PDF.

Scanned document?

A scan has no text to translate, only an image. Run it through OCR with the Searchable PDF output first, then translate the result.

Tips for a better translation

  • Check names and numbers. Machine translation sometimes "translates" personal or company names. Make sure amounts, dates and clause numbers match the original.
  • Watch professional terms. A legal or medical term can be rendered as an everyday word that changes the meaning. If something sounds odd, look at the term in the original.
  • Cramped layout? Translations are often longer than the source. If the result looks squeezed, run it again with layout off.
