A hospital discharge letter, a visit summary, lab results: there are plenty of times you need a medical document in another language, or need to understand one that arrived from abroad. Here's how to translate it in minutes, what you must check afterwards, and when you need a certified translator.

When you need to translate a medical document

  • A second opinion abroad: the doctor there needs your summaries and test results in English.
  • Treatment abroad or a travel insurance claim: hospital documents you need to understand or submit to your insurer.
  • A new doctor or a move abroad: your medical history for a new physician.
  • Understanding what it says: a document from abroad, before you talk to your own doctor.

How to translate

  1. Paper document? Scan or photograph it. The phone scanning guide shows how to get a clean PDF.
  2. A scan is an image without text. Run it through OCR first to get a PDF with real text.
  3. Open Translate PDF, upload the file and pick the target language.
  4. Keep the original layout on so lab result tables stay in place.
  5. Download the file and go through the checklist below.

What to check after machine translation

In a medical document a small slip can change the meaning. Compare these against the original:

  • Drug names: sometimes translated as ordinary words or mangled. A drug name should match the original exactly.
  • Doses and units: mg and mcg differ by a factor of a thousand. Make sure every number and unit came through unchanged.
  • Dates: many countries write day/month, the US writes month/day. 03/05 can be 3 May or March 5. Write the month in words for a US doctor.
  • Lab values: numbers, reference ranges and flags such as H or L must match the original.
  • Abbreviations: medical shorthand doesn't always translate well. If something looks odd, keep the original term in brackets.

When machine translation isn't enough

For understanding and personal use, machine translation with a careful check is usually fine. However:

  • Insurers, authorities and courts sometimes require a certified or notarised translation. Ask exactly what they need first.
  • Medical decisions are made with a doctor, not from a translation. The translation helps you ask the right questions.

Even when you need a certified translator, machine translation helps: you understand roughly what the document says and can check the official version didn't miss anything. The guide to translating official documents covers certified translation.

Privacy

Medical records are among the most sensitive documents there are. Files are deleted from our server after one hour, and the text is sent to a machine translation service to be translated. If the translation doesn't need your ID number or address, hide them first with Redact PDF.

In short

Scan, run OCR if needed, translate with the layout kept, and check drugs, doses, dates and values against the original. For official submissions, find out whether a certified translator is required.
