How to Translate a PDF or Contract and Keep Its Layout

October 11, 2026

You have a document in another language, a product manual, a letter from a company abroad or a syllabus, and want to read it in your own language without pasting it into a translation site paragraph by paragraph. You can translate the whole PDF at once and get it back as a PDF with the same design. Here's how, and what to expect from the result.

How to translate a PDF

  1. Open Translate PDF and choose the file.
  2. Under "Translate to", pick the language. The source language is detected automatically.
  3. Leave "Keep original layout" on "Yes" and click translate.
  4. Download the translated PDF. A document of a few pages usually takes under a minute.

It works between Hebrew, English, Arabic, Russian, French, German and Spanish, in either direction.

What happens to the design

With layout kept, every translated line goes back where the original line was, in the same colour, size and weight (a bold heading stays bold). Images, logos and lines don't move. Right-to-left languages are written right to left, with numbers, prices and English abbreviations kept in the right order inside them.

A translation can come out shorter or longer than the original. When it is longer, the text is scaled down slightly to fit the same box. If the result looks cramped, for example in a table with small cells, run it again with "No, plain text" for a clean, easy-to-read document.

Which PDFs work

The tool translates real text. To check, try selecting a word in the PDF. If you can, you're fine. If the file is a scan or a photo, it has no text to translate: run it through OCR first, choose "Searchable PDF" as the output, and translate the result.

How accurate is it

Translation is done by a language model, not word by word, so sentences read naturally and names of people, products and dishes are written out rather than "translated". For everyday documents, such as letters, instructions or articles, the result is good enough to understand everything and act on it.

Official documents, such as certificates, court rulings or papers you submit to an authority, need a certified translator and sometimes a notary. There's more in translating an official document: when machine translation is enough.

Translating a contract

A common use: you received a contract in another language, an employment contract with an international company, a lease abroad or a supplier's terms, and want to understand what you're signing. The translation gives you a good picture of the content, but with a contract a few things are worth checking against the original, line by line:

  • Amounts, currencies and dates. Make sure the numbers in the translation match the original. Watch the date format: 03/04/2027 in a US contract is 4 March, not 3 April.
  • Defined terms. In English contracts, words like the Company or Confidential Information are defined near the start, and that definition, not the dictionary, sets their meaning throughout.
  • Obligation or option. shall and must are obligations, may is an option. That difference changes the whole clause, so check the translation kept it.
  • Legal terms. Words like indemnify, liability or consideration have precise legal meanings that a general translation can soften.
  • The clauses that matter most: termination and notice period, automatic renewal, penalties, limitation of liability, and which law applies and where disputes are heard.

The binding version is always the original contract, not the translation. For a contract with significant amounts, such as employment or an investment, it's worth showing it to a lawyer before signing. The translation helps you get to that meeting with the right questions.

Is the document in Word, not PDF?

Convert it first with Word to PDF, then translate. If you need to edit the text after translating, PDF to Word turns the translated document back into Word, with right-to-left text in the right order.

Privacy

The text is sent to a translation service to be translated, and files are deleted from our server after one hour. Full details, including the provider, are in the privacy policy. If the document contains an ID or account number that doesn't need translating, you can black it out first with Redact PDF.
