How to Translate an Academic Paper PDF (for Students)

November 23, 2026

Your lecturer posted three papers for next week, 20 pages each, and they're not in your first language. Translating a whole paper gets you the big picture fast, so you can go back to the original for the parts that matter. Here's how to do it well.

First: check the paper has text

Papers downloaded from journal sites and university libraries almost always contain real text. Try selecting a sentence with the mouse: if it highlights, it can be translated. If the paper is a scan of a book or a library photocopy, run it through OCR first.

How to translate

  1. Open Translate PDF, upload the paper and pick your language.
  2. Choose whether to keep the layout:
    • Yes: the translation goes back in the same place on the page, tables and headings included. Good for comparing page by page.
    • No, plain text: the translation flows in a single column. For a two-column paper this is usually easier to read.
  3. Download the file and read it next to the original.

A very long paper? Split it first and translate only the sections you need, such as the introduction, discussion and conclusion.

What machine translation does well, and less well

  • Well: getting the main argument, the method and the conclusions, and quickly finding the parts relevant to your work.
  • Less well: technical terms. A word like "significant" in statistics can come out as "important" instead of "statistically significant". When a term looks odd, check the original.
  • Not translated: formulas, charts and bibliography entries stay as they are, which is fine: you cite the sources in their original form anyway.

Academic integrity

Translation helps you understand, but the work has to be yours. Cite the original paper, not the translation, and don't paste translated paragraphs into your assignment without quotation marks and a reference. If in doubt, check your course rules.

The other direction: an English abstract

Need an English abstract for a seminar paper or thesis? Translating your text gives you a first draft. Then go over it, fix the terms and polish the wording. For anything you submit, have someone with strong English read it.

Other tools students use

  • Audio to text: record a lecture and get searchable text to summarise.
  • Merge PDF: combine all of a course's readings into one file.
  • PDF to Word: when you want to copy a quote or a table into your paper.

In short

Check there's text, translate with or without the layout depending on the paper, read the translation next to the original, and always cite the original.
