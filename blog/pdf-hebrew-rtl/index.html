Why Hebrew Text Gets Scrambled in PDF Conversion (and the Fix)

July 1, 2026

You convert a Hebrew PDF to Word, or copy text out of it, and get "םולש" instead of "שלום", sentences with the words in reverse order, or brackets and numbers in the wrong place. It's the most common problem with Hebrew (and Arabic) documents. Here's why it happens, how to tell in ten seconds what kind of file you have, and how to get a correct one.

What it looks like

  • Letters reversed inside a word: "םולש" instead of "שלום". The clearest sign.
  • Words in reverse order: each word is fine, but the sentence reads from the end.
  • Numbers, English and brackets in the wrong place: "(2026)" turns into ")2026(", or an English word jumps to the end of the line.
  • Left alignment: paragraphs stick to the left and the full stop appears at the start of the line.

Why it happens

A PDF doesn't store sentences. It stores letters and where each one sits on the page. A converter, or your copy and paste, has to rebuild the reading order from those positions. In English that is simple: left to right. Hebrew has two problems:

  • Some PDFs store Hebrew in reverse. Older software, some company systems and some virtual printers write letters in the order they appear on screen, left to right. Anything that reads them in stored order gets a reversed word.
  • Lines that mix Hebrew, English and numbers need the Unicode bidirectional rules to come out right. A converter that doesn't apply them lays the whole line out in one direction.

A ten-second test: which PDF do you have

Open the PDF, select one line, copy it and paste it into a text editor or a new message.

  • The text comes out right: the file is fine and the converter was the problem. Convert again with a tool that supports Hebrew.
  • The text comes out reversed: the file itself stores Hebrew in reverse. A good converter detects and reorders it, but copying straight from the file will always come out reversed.
  • Nothing can be selected: it's a scan or an image with no text in it. You need OCR (below).

How to convert a Hebrew PDF to Word correctly

  1. Open PDF to Word and choose the file.
  2. Download the Word file. The converter detects Hebrew paragraphs, sets them right to left, and orders mixed lines so numbers and English words stay in place.
  3. Check one paragraph that has a number or an English word in it. If it's right, the whole document most likely is.

If it's a scan

A scan has no text, only a picture of text. Run it through OCR with "Hebrew + English" and choose "Word document" as the output. The text comes back right to left and in the right order. Clear scans are recognised very accurately; blurry photos and handwriting are worth checking.

I already have a Word file that came out reversed

  • Only the alignment or the punctuation is wrong: select the paragraphs in Word and press Ctrl + right Shift. That makes the paragraph right to left, and full stops and brackets go back where they belong.
  • The letters themselves are reversed: there's no quick manual fix. Go back to the original PDF and convert it again with a tool that supports Hebrew. It takes less time than fixing one page by hand.
